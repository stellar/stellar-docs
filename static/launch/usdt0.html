Transfer USDT0 to Stellar →
Balances move into and out of Stellar without a custodial bridge or a wrapped representation. Transfer time is set by the chain you send from — a couple of minutes from some networks, hours from others. The cost is that network's gas plus the LayerZero message, quoted per route before you send. USDT0 itself charges no protocol fee on most routes; where one applies it is 0.03%, set per route. Receiving USDT0 needs a trustline on the destination account — see requirements and limitations.

Before you build — requirements and limitations

What USDT0 on Stellar requires for integration.

Trustlines

A trustline has to exist before the first payment lands. Sending USDT0 to an account that has no USDT0 trustline fails with op_no_trust. A wallet may create it for you when you swap into USDT0 in-app; a transfer arriving from an exchange or another wallet has no such fallback. If you custody for users, create it during onboarding — sponsored reserves can cover the reserve.

Decimals

The OFT reconciles a precision gap for you. USDT0 on Stellar carries seven decimals; cross-chain transfers normalize to the OFT’s six shared decimals. Send 1.2345678 and 1.234567 moves; the remaining 0.0000008 is floored off as dust and refunded to you before the debit. Nothing to convert by hand — but account for it when you calculate or display an amount.

Route limits

A route can enforce a minimum. Minimum transfer amounts are set per contract, so call quote_oft for the route you intend to use rather than assuming any amount will go through.

Backing

The backing sits in one contract. USDT0's adapter on Ethereum holds the USDT behind the natively issued supply.

Yield

USDT0 doesn't accrue yield. It earns nothing sitting in a balance — any return has to come from the market you supply it to.

Listings

Each venue decides which networks it supports. One USDT0 doesn't mean every listing offers every route, so check what a partner actually enables before you send users to it.

FAQ

Is USDT0 the same as USDT?

No — it is a separate asset with its own contract, worth the same and backed 1:1 by USDT locked on Ethereum. The practical difference is deposits: a venue that supports USDT will not necessarily accept USDT0 on Stellar. Check your destination lists USDT0 on Stellar specifically before you send.

Which USDT0 on Stellar is the real one?

The one issued by GATISXX6BZ6NC7IKQBY37CJD4SOZL3CYZJWXEDG6JVIY4WBS6KXJHN6Q — check it on Stellar Expert. Asset codes on Stellar are not unique — anyone can issue an asset called USDT0 — so identity is the code and the issuer together. Pin that issuer, and confirm the contract you derive from it matches CBSJZEIO5C7KC2SF3MKSNXXJSW5G3VTNBX4ATMKUI3B2MR4JKM4R26YF.

Why did my USDT0 payment fail with op_no_trust?

The destination account has no USDT0 trustline, and one must exist before the first payment can land. A wallet often creates it when a user swaps into USDT0 in-app, but a transfer arriving from an exchange or another wallet has no such fallback. If you custody for users, create it during onboarding — sponsored reserves can cover the reserve.

How long does a transfer to Stellar take?

It depends on the chain you send from, not on Stellar. Stellar finalises in 5–7 seconds; the wait is the source chain confirming and LayerZero verifying the message. From Polygon it is about a minute. From Ethereum, Arbitrum, Tron and Solana — the routes carrying most volume — expect roughly 18–21 minutes. A few low-traffic routes take hours. The transfer UI quotes an estimate for your route before you send.

Why is my transfer slower than I expected?

Usually because the figure you saw was a network-wide best case rather than your route. Nothing on the Stellar side is the bottleneck. If a transfer runs well past the estimate you were quoted, look up the message on LayerZero Scan, which indexes Stellar as a destination, to see which step it is sitting at. USDT0 lists support contacts for transfers that stall.

What does it cost to bring USDT0 to Stellar?

Three separate things, and two different calls report them. USDT0's own protocol fee is zero on most routes and 0.03% where one applies: quote_oft shows it as the gap between amount_sent_ld and amount_received_ld, so read it there rather than trusting a published number. The LayerZero messaging fee is separate and comes back from quote_send, paid in XLM when you send from Stellar — this is the part that varies most. And on Stellar, ordinary transactions cost a minimum of 100 stroops per operation, which is unrelated to bridging.

What is the difference between the Open Mesh and the Legacy Mesh?

On the Open Mesh each chain runs its own OFT contract, and moving between them burns on one side and mints on the other. The Legacy Mesh connects chains where Tether had already deployed native USDT — Ethereum, Tron, TON, Arbitrum, Solana and Celo — through a shared non-custodial hub on Arbitrum, so those chains keep their existing USDT contracts rather than migrating.

Why did I receive slightly less than I sent?

Rounding, in the last decimal places only. USDT0 on Stellar carries seven decimals; cross-chain transfers normalise to the OFT's six shared decimals. Send 1.2345678 and 1.234567 moves — the remaining 0.0000008 is floored off as dust and refunded to you before the debit.

Who can freeze or clawback USDT0 on Stellar?

The issuer. The asset sets auth_revocable and auth_clawback_enabled, so the issuer can revoke a trustline or clawback a balance. auth_required is not set, so anyone can hold it without approval.
